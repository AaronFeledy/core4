# Share a service or extra port

`lando share` opens a public tunnel to the current app. Omit `--target` and Lando uses the first route from the app plan.

```sh
lando share
```

Storybook is not the homepage. Point `--target` at it.

## Point `--target` at a service port

```yaml
name: share-target-demo
services:
  app:
    type: "node:22"
    primary: true
  storybook:
    type: "node:22"
```

```sh
lando share --target storybook:6006
```

`--target` takes `service[:port]`. Vite on the primary service is the same move: `lando share --target app:5173`.

## Point `--target` at a route or loopback URL

```sh
lando share --target storybook.share-target-demo.lndo.site
lando share --target http://127.0.0.1:5173
```

`http://` or `https://` means a loopback URL. `storybook:6006` is a service and port. Anything else is a route id, usually the hostname `lando info` prints. Drop the port and `lando share --target storybook` is a route id, not a service.

## See what will be shared

```sh
lando start
lando info
lando share --help
```

`info` prints services and URLs. The default share target is the first of those routes. `--help` restates the three `--target` forms.

## List and stop tunnels

```sh
lando share:list
lando share:stop <session>
```

`lando share list` and `lando share stop <session>` are the same commands. `--session` is the flag form of the session id. `--force` asks the provider to force-stop when it supports that.

`--detach` records a tunnel as a detached session. `--provider` selects a TunnelService plugin id. `--yes` answers confirmation prompts.

## When no tunnel plugin is installed

```sh
lando plugin:add <tunnel-service-plugin>
```

Or `lando setup --provider=<provider-with-tunnels>`. Then rerun `lando share`. Without a plugin, `share`, `share:list`, and `share:stop` fail with `TunnelProviderUnavailableError`. Bundled tunnel connectors ship in Lando 4.1.
